Lester J. Marston, Cahuilla

A lifetime of advocacy in Indian country

Following his graduation from UC Hastings College of Law, Mr. Marston began his law career in the 1970s as a staff and directing attorney at California Indian Legal Services. He entered private practice in 1982.

Mr. Marston has served as in-house counsel to sixteen federally recognized Indian tribes in California, Arizona, Oregon, and Wisconsin. He has also worked with tribes, Indian organizations, and groups in Kansas, Michigan, New York, Minnesota, and Washington.

For five years, he served as Chief Judge for the Confederated Tribes of the Grand Ronde Indian Community of Oregon. He also served for more than ten years as Chief Judge of the Blue Lake Rancheria Tribal Court.

Selected case history

Decades of consequential legal work

Federal recognition

Tillie Hardwick, et al. v. United States

While at California Indian Legal Services, Mr. Marston worked on the seminal Tillie Hardwick case. The settlement restored federal recognition to 17 California Indian tribes and restored their tribal land bases.

Ninth Circuit victory

Chicken Ranch Rancheria v. State of California

Mr. Marston and his legal team prevailed against the State of California and Governor Gavin Newsom in Chicken Ranch Rancheria v. Newsom, 42 F.4th 1024 (9th Cir. 2022).

The Ninth Circuit held that California and its governor failed to negotiate tribal-state gaming compacts in good faith by demanding provisions unrelated to gaming operations and outside the permissible scope of negotiation under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act.

Legal services

Experienced counsel for tribal governments and tribal entities

Mr. Marston and his team advise tribal clients on major impact litigation, government operations, transactions, negotiations, and legislation that affect Indian tribes and Indian people.

In-House Counsel

Mr. Marston and his legal team serve as in-house counsel to tribal governments and tribal entities. Their work includes legal services for tax commissions, tribal court systems, police departments, housing authorities, fish and game departments, economic development corporations, gaming commissions, and tribal joint powers agencies.

They regularly advise tribal councils and governmental arms, draft agreements and contracts, and have worked on hundreds of fee-to-trust land acquisitions.

Special Counsel

The firm acts as special counsel on individual matters, including tribal water rights negotiations and litigation. Mr. Marston was one of four lawyers chosen to represent 64 California Indian tribes in negotiations that produced the 1999 tribal-state gaming compacts.

The team drafts compacts, management agreements, equipment leases, consulting agreements, and documents used to finance and develop gaming facilities, hotels, and related facilities.

Legislative Work

Mr. Marston and his team have worked on significant legislation, including the 1988 amendments to the Indian Reorganization Act, California Fish and Game Code sections authorizing fishing agreements with Indian tribes, and the Tribal Court Civil Money Judgment Act.

That California law requires state courts to recognize and enforce tribal court civil money judgments.
